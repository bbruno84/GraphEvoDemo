# V01 T5 — separate local owners, unified fresh-peer recovery

Classification: **observed under the stated conditions**. Two independent
one-store owners kept their local A/B fixture distributions separate during
the observation window, but a fresh single-store peer recovered the exact full
A+B fixture. Local file/owner separation did not demonstrate separate cloud
recovery domains in this tested topology. No v2 architecture is selected.

## Question and configuration

After the [T0 control](v01-t0-2026-10-23.md) and
[T1 native rejection](v01-t1-2026-10-23.md), the maintainer explicitly authorized
two distinct `NSPersistentCloudKitContainer` instances, each opening its own
fresh SQLite file, with the identical T0 model and the same dedicated CloudKit
Development resource/private scope. Both writer owners stayed alive throughout
the sampling window. Each owner had exactly one store; the T1 duplicate
resource/scope guard was not weakened or bypassed for a multi-store owner.

Fixture A-001/alpha and A-002/beta were inserted only through owner-A; B-001/gamma
only through owner-B. Each private-context snapshot selected its exact store
with `affectedStores` and filtered only by runID. The independent iPad reader
opened one fresh SQLite path and performed no fixture insert/restore/copy or
logicalGraphID filtering. No custom zone, direct CKRecord write, scope change,
fallback, schema initialization, remote wipe or uninstall was performed.
See the [T5 guide](../features/v01-independent-owners.md).

## Interpretation, limits and cleanup

The fresh peer recovered A and B together in one local replica. Therefore,
opening distinct SQLite files through distinct owners with these settings did
not yield independently selective recovery in the generic fresh peer tested.
The writer's separate local rows alone would have been insufficient evidence
for cloud separation. This finding is limited to this model, private scope,
resource, devices/account, OS builds, fixture and window; it is not a universal
statement about every supported container, zone or sharing arrangement.

No graph-specific peer selection mechanism, different account, sharing,
concurrent edits, reversed opening order, offline lifecycle or larger workload
was tested here. T1 remains natively rejected, T2 remains unexecuted, and T3/T4
were not performed. No topology workaround or library change follows
automatically from this result; further choices return to design review.

Both app processes were explicitly ended after evidence retention. No store
file was deleted, no remote records were purged, and no production resource
was touched. Local laboratory stores/reports and remote synthetic fixtures
remain for review. A later cleanup must be explicit and confined to the
verified authorized Development resource; closing an owner is not remote cleanup.
